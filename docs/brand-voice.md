# Sodapop's bubbly-sidekick voice

Sodapop sounds like a coding companion who's pleased you brought a project.
Affectionate, a bit cheeky, and comfortable around a codebase. Give the copy
some personality instead of explaining that the product has personality.

This is an authoring reference, not a published user guide. Keep it outside
the website's public-content map.

## The recipe

Use a playful invitation, a concrete explanation, and an obvious action.

> A little can of "let's build that."

Follow a headline like this with what Sodapop is: an independent terminal coding
companion powered by the GitHub Copilot SDK. Describe something a developer can
do, such as exploring code, working through a change, or inspecting a diff.
Then offer a literal action such as "Get Sodapop" or "Read the docs."

Give each short section one playful hook. The next sentence should help the
reader, not serve up another slogan.

## Words with somewhere to go

| Instead of | Try | Why it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A fresh take on your terminal | Your repo called. It ordered something fizzy. | Makes the joke while keeping the repo in view. |
| Comfortably familiar. Refreshingly different. | Let's poke around your repo. | Invites a specific activity. |
| Less hunting. More doing. | The good stuff starts with `/`. | Leads directly to command discovery. |
| Make yourself at home. | How fizzy are we feeling? | Introduces actual theme and personality choices. |
| Small delights, built in. | Pairs well with side projects. | Gives the mascot something to say. |

"First sip" belongs near getting started. "Flavor" and "fizz" fit customization.
Not every feature needs a drink metaphor. Keep code, repos, prompts, themes,
and diffs in the vocabulary.

Skip vague praise such as "real tools," "seamless," and "refreshingly different."
Avoid repeating "Less X. More Y." contrasts. Do not ban a word mechanically:
explaining what a recording actually shows matters more than avoiding "real."

## Turn the fizz down when it helps

| Surface | Tone |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage, mascot tags, closing | Warm and playful; follow the hook with a concrete detail. |
| Docs welcome and getting started | Friendly orientation around exact, usable steps. |
| Customization | Room for "How fizzy are we feeling?" followed by `/theme` and its settings. |
| Commands and installation | A brief welcome, then literal names, prerequisites, and instructions. |
| Troubleshooting | Calm and specific about the problem and the next action. No blame or teasing. |
| Permissions, credentials, cancellation | Direct. No jokes or reassuring slogans in place of the facts. |

Keep navigation and accessible labels recognizable: "Getting started,"
"Installation," "Customization," and "Copy installation command." A metaphor
can introduce a guide; it must not become the only way to find or use it.
Use sentence case and existing US spelling.

## Facts are not flavor

Preserve the distinctions in [permissions and privacy](permissions-and-privacy.md),
[installation](installation.md), and [customization](customization.md). Planning
is advisory, approved shell commands are not sandboxed, and cancellation does
not undo completed edits. Personality settings change presentation, not model
capabilities or approval rules.

Keep account requirements, release availability, platform support, and demo
disclosures precise. A signed-out recording with prepared changes is not a live
AI interaction. A source-build option is not a published installer.

Do not invent founder stories, testimonials, speed claims, or guaranteed
results. Use original language, not borrowed lyrics or another property's
branding. Keep Sodapop's independent identity and upstream attribution clear.

Before publishing, read the copy aloud. Cut any line that only announces how
delightful the app is. Check that a reader can still tell what it does and what
to do next, even if they skip every soda joke.
